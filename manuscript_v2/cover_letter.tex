\vspace{0.5cm}
Dear Editors,

\vspace{0.2cm}
The manuscript \textbf{``Relative effects of seagrass canopy, wave coupling and bed mobility on Lagrangian transport in a shallow lagoon''}, by Cicero Martins Jr., Mauro De Marchis, Giuseppe Ciraolo, Carmelo Nasello, Antonino Maltese, is
submitted for consideration as an original research paper. The corresponding
author is MSc.\ Cicero Martins Jr.

\vspace{0.3cm}
Coastal lagoon models are commonly validated against water level and then used
to describe transport. The manuscript asks which modelled processes control
transport in a shallow vegetated lagoon and whether water level can identify
them. A complete factorial in wave coupling, seagrass canopy and bed mobility
gives eight configurations of a three-dimensional Delft3D Flexible Mesh model
coupled online with SWAN, each scored against three tide gauges and 35 GPS
surface drifters over the same nine days.

On the fixed bed the seagrass canopy raises Liu--Weisberg skill by 0.120 and
0.114, with both bootstrap intervals clear of zero, against $-0.022$ and
$-0.028$ for wave coupling. The canopy is represented as a three-dimensional
drag field whose parameters come from published measurement of the two species,
with nothing fitted. Water level separates the canopy treatments only at the
gauge inside the vegetated basin, where anomaly RMSE differs by 26 and 27~mm,
against at most 4~mm at the two gauges near the inlets.

The mobile-bed arm shows how an attribution can mislead. With waves, the
uncalibrated bed lets a configuration without a canopy reach the skill of the
vegetated ones, through a bathymetry reshaped by erosion that holds near
9~g~l$^{-1}$ of suspended sediment in the lagoon. A segment with the
bed held fixed over the drifter window keeps the same skill, which locates the
gain in the reshaped bed rather than in any transport process, and the interior
gauge exposes the compensation where the drifters do not. A start from a
uniform offshore column inflates the canopy effect in the same way, from 0.12
to 0.19.

The work falls within the scope of \textit{Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf
Science} as a study of lagoon phenomena and of coastal sedimentary processes.
Although the model is built for one basin, the questions it answers concern
what a class of validation data can certify and how a factorial behaves when a
dominant factor is left out or another is unconstrained, and both apply wherever
drifter observations and a circulation model exist together. The prior study
closest to the wave treatment, Carniello et al.\ (2011), appeared in this
journal. The limitations are stated in the manuscript, including the
uncalibrated morphodynamic arm, kept out of the attribution for that reason,
and the single nine-day window.
