\section{Summary}

Work on the coupled three-dimensional model resumed on 5 August after a pause
since mid-June, due to the rewriting of the manuscripts related to GEE. As we
already had several runs done and the skeleton of the paper in place, the main
outcome of the first three days was to compile everything and finalise the
first draft, in LaTeX and formatted for \textit{Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf
Science}.

Then the experimental design changed under us, for the better. Two of the eight
cells of the factorial had been failing to run, and tracing that failure to its
cause let both complete. Their results reverse the paper's central finding.
Sections 2 and 3 below are the new picture; the draft has been rewritten
accordingly and is ready for your reading.

\section{What changed, and why it matters}

The design crosses three factors: wave coupling, bed mobility, and whether
seagrass resistance is uniform or distributed from a satellite classification.
Until yesterday the two cells combining a mobile bed with no wave coupling could
not be obtained. The cause turned out to be numerical rather than physical, the
contribution of suspended sediment to the density field, and disabling it let
both members run to completion with no other change.

With those two cells in hand the attribution comes out differently. On a fixed
bed wave coupling costs 0.035 to 0.042 in Liu--Weisberg skill. On a mobile bed
the same treatment \emph{gains} 0.155 and 0.232. Bed mobility behaves
symmetrically: without waves it costs 0.071 to 0.075 and makes every one of the
35 drifters worse, in both roughness treatments; with waves it gains 0.119 to
0.199.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Change in Liu--Weisberg skill & On a fixed bed & On a mobile bed \\
\midrule
From adding wave coupling & $-0.035$ to $-0.042$ & $+0.155$ to $+0.232$ \\
\midrule
                          & \textbf{Without waves} & \textbf{With waves} \\
From adding bed mobility  & $-0.071$ to $-0.075$ & $+0.119$ to $+0.199$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So neither treatment improves prediction on its own, and each is what makes the
other work. Applied singly they are the two treatments that make the model
worse. The two worst and the two best members of the ensemble are built from the
same pair and differ only in whether both are switched on. All eight bootstrap
intervals behind those numbers are clear of zero.

Distributed seagrass roughness has moved to third order on this evidence. It
changes skill by 0.009 or less in three of its four conditions, and by 0.081 in
the fourth, which is on a mobile bed with wave coupling. Its effect on the wave
field remains real and correctly ordered by canopy density, reducing near-bed
orbital velocity by 14.3\% over \emph{Posidonia} against 3.3\% over exposed
rock, so the parametrisation works. It simply does not move surface transport
unless the other two are present.

\section{What is unchanged}

Two results survive the reanalysis and support the framing. Water level still cannot
separate the configurations, and the case is stronger with eight members than
with six: anomaly RMSE differs by at most 4.2~mm at any station, against a
Lagrangian skill range of 0.469 to 0.705 and endpoint separation of 336 to
625~m. And surface current speed exceeds bed speed by a factor of 1.85 to 2.20
at sub-metre mean depth, which is the quantitative case for solving in three
dimensions, consistent with De~Marchis et al.\ (2012) here.

One earlier number was corrected in the process. The vertical shear ratio had
appeared to reach 4.5 in the mobile-bed members; that was an artefact of the
same sediment-density coupling suppressing near-bed flow, and the true range
across all eight members is the narrower one quoted above.

\section{Two questions for you}

\textbf{First, the title.} The one in the draft is my current preference, but
the other two are live and I would value your view.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \emph{Interacting effects of wave coupling and bed mobility on
  Lagrangian transport in a shallow vegetated lagoon.} In the draft. Conservative
  and conventional in form.
  \item \emph{Wave coupling helps or hurts lagoon transport prediction depending
  on whether the bed can move.} States the finding rather than the topic, and the
  conditional form claims no more than the eight cells show.
  \item \emph{Transport validation of a shallow lagoon model: interacting
  effects of wave coupling and bed mobility.} Method first, mechanism second.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Second, and more consequential, whether to submit before obtaining a new
bathymetric survey.} The configurations that best reproduce observed transport
are the ones with a mobile bed, and its critical shear stress for erosion has
not been constrained against measured bathymetric change. We are using the old
bathymetry, so it is possible that a mobile bed helps because it moves the old
morphology towards the present one, in which case the improvement is a
compensation rather than a physical result. The manuscript states this openly,
and notes that the lagoon bed changes by up to 1.4~m over nine days in isolated
cells, which is not a plausible rate. If there is a realistic chance of
obtaining new bathymetry soon, I would rather wait and rerun with it than defend
the present version in review.

\section{Status and plan}

The draft is complete across all six sections, with eight figures and three
tables, 5950 words against a limit of 8000, and 24 references. Highlights and a
cover letter are drafted. Outstanding is a graphical abstract, which
\textit{ECSS} requires.

After the holidays, and before leaving for Malta, my intention is to close this
paper and begin drafting the remaining themes we discussed: the effect of the
multi-decadal \emph{Posidonia} decline on wave attenuation, near-bed exposure and
residence time; the operational forecast chain with the WetWise portal, together
with the Isola Grande channel reopening scenario; and wave-driven sediment
mobilisation with the Trapani canal contribution to lagoon siltation.

The full draft, figures and analysis code are in the project repository, and I
can send the compiled PDF on request.
