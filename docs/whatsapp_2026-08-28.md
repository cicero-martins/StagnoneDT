Buongiorno Professori. I have a significant correction to the August report,
and I think it improves the paper rather than damaging it.

The conclusion that distributed seagrass roughness is a third-order effect was
wrong. The seagrass was never actually resisting the flow. Two separate faults:
our roughness file was written with six decimals against a 1 cm matching
tolerance, so 94% of the meadow was silently discarded; and the Baptist
formulation in D-Flow FM computes a canopy momentum sink that the solver never
reads, which I proved with two runs that are bit-identical with the drag
coefficient at 0 and at 0.8.

Rebuilt on FM's native vegetation module, with every parameter taken from
published measurement and nothing fitted, distributed roughness becomes the
strongest single effect in the ensemble instead of the weakest. It now helps in
all four configurations, not one, and the configuration where the August report
said it acts turns out to be where it acts least.

Professor Ciraolo, the calibration rests on your 2006 flume paper. Using your
canopy thickness rather than the leaf length, the modelled meadow resistance
lands within 3% of your Equation 22, evaluated at the model's own velocities,
with nothing tuned against it. The drifters were held out entirely and confirm
it afterwards.

There is also a direct answer to the bathymetry question from August, and it is
not the one I expected. The wave and bed-mobility synergy we saw exists only
when the meadow is missing. Details in the report.

Sections 4.3 and 5 need rewriting. Two questions for you inside, and I would
value a call this week if you have time.
