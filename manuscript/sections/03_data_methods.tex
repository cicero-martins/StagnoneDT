\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data_methods}

\subsection{Hydrodynamic model}
\label{sec:methods_model}

Circulation was simulated with D-Flow Flexible Mesh (D-Flow FM, kernel
1.2.184) from the Delft3D FM Suite 2026.01, an unstructured finite-volume
solver for the three-dimensional shallow-water equations \citep{Kernkamp2011}.
The computational mesh comprised 21{,}188 nodes and 25{,}212 predominantly
quadrilateral faces, and covered the lagoon together with an offshore
extension reaching the western Sicilian channel, spanning
11.96--12.56$^\circ$E and 37.70--38.06$^\circ$N in geographic (WGS84)
coordinates. Resolution was graded from the offshore boundary towards the
lagoon interior, where the inlets and the intertidal flats required the finest
cells. The vertical was discretised with 10 sigma layers
(\texttt{SigmaGrowthFactor} 1.2), which distributes resolution towards the bed
while retaining a terrain-following representation appropriate to a basin
whose depth varies from a few centimetres to about 3~m.

Turbulence closure used the $k$--$\varepsilon$ model with background vertical
eddy viscosity and diffusivity of $10^{-4}$~\si{\metre\squared\per\second} and
uniform horizontal values of 0.1~\si{\metre\squared\per\second}. Density was
computed from the UNESCO equation of state, with salinity and temperature both
transported as active constituents so that the summer hypersaline gradient
contributes baroclinically to the momentum balance. Bed levels were specified
at cell centres from the bathymetry file. Bed roughness was prescribed as a
Manning coefficient, uniform at 0.023~s~m$^{-1/3}$ in the reference
configuration and spatially variable in the vegetated configurations
(Section~\ref{sec:methods_roughness}). Wind drag followed a linear
Charnock-type formulation with a drag coefficient of 0.025.

The simulation covered nine days, from 1~July 2025 00:00 to 10~July 2025 00:00
UTC. External forcing was updated every 300~s. The computational time step was
adaptive under a Courant limit of 0.7, capped at 30~s and constrained to grow
by no more than a factor 1.1 between successive steps. Initial conditions were
ramped over the first hour. The domain was decomposed into eight partitions
and run under MPI.

\subsection{Wave model and coupling}
\label{sec:methods_waves}

Waves were computed with SWAN \citep{Booij1999} through the Delft3D-WAVE
module, on two nested curvilinear grids. The outer grid covered the full
D-Flow FM domain at approximately 400~m resolution and supplied boundary
conditions to an inner grid of approximately 100~m resolution over the lagoon
and its inlets. The spectral space was discretised into 24 directions over the
full circle and 24 frequencies logarithmically distributed between 0.05 and
1.0~Hz.

Third-generation source terms were used, with Komen whitecapping,
quadruplet wave--wave interactions, refraction, and frequency shifting all
active. Triad interactions and wave setup were disabled. Depth-induced
breaking followed the Battjes--Janssen formulation with $\alpha = 1.0$ and
$\gamma = 0.73$, and bottom friction used the JONSWAP formulation with a
coefficient of 0.067~\si{\metre\squared\per\cubic\second}. A minimum depth of
0.05~m was imposed. Wave forces were passed to the flow solver as
three-dimensional dissipation.

Flow and waves were coupled online through the DIMR framework, exchanging
fields every 3600~s. Within each exchange the wave model ran in stationary
mode, so the time variation of the wave field followed from the re-evaluated
water levels, currents, wind, and boundary spectra rather than from spectral
propagation in time. Communication files were written in classic NetCDF
(\texttt{ncFormat = 3}), which was required for the wave module to re-open the
communication file reliably across the coupled run.

\subsection{Boundary and atmospheric forcing}
\label{sec:methods_forcing}

Open-sea boundaries were forced with the CMEMS Mediterranean Sea physical
reanalysis. Water level, salinity, and temperature were prescribed along the
offshore boundary, together with a depth-resolved advection velocity
(\texttt{uxuyadvectionvelocitybnd}). Prescribing the advection velocity was
necessary for stability in the deep offshore cells, where a water-level-only
boundary admitted spurious accelerations.

The CMEMS sea surface height field is an anomaly about the model rest state
rather than a geodetic level, so it cannot be imposed directly as a free
surface without a reference correction. A per-node offset was therefore added
to the water-level boundary, anchored on the tide gauge at Marettimo island
(JRC TAD 658) and distributed along the boundary using the CMEMS mean dynamic
topography. The anchor was estimated from a thirteen-month record spanning
January 2025 to January 2026.

Atmospheric forcing combined ERA5 reanalysis with in-situ observation. Wind
components, mean sea level pressure, and the Charnock parameter were taken
from ERA5, and the wind field was blended with the record from the
AltaVilaEst station. Earlier configurations that additionally blended a second
in-situ station produced a systematic rotation of the wind vector along the
eastern lagoon margin, so the AltaVilaEst record alone was retained.
Evaporation was imposed through the rainfall quantity with a negative sign,
which is the convention D-Flow FM expects, with both the rainfall and
evaporation switches enabled in the model definition. Interior salinity and
temperature were nudged towards the CMEMS fields with a relaxation time of
$8.64\times10^{5}$~s, long enough to leave the lagoon interior essentially
free-running while constraining the offshore extension.

Initial conditions represented the observed summer state, with a hypersaline
field prescribed over the lagoon. Three passive tracers were carried, one
labelling the lagoon water mass and two representing the discharges from the
Trapani airport and saltpan canal systems.

\subsection{Seagrass roughness parametrisation}
\label{sec:methods_roughness}

Spatially variable bed resistance was applied through the trachytope
formulation, which assigns a roughness class to each flow link and combines
classes within a computational cell by area weighting. Four classes were
defined. Unvegetated substrate was represented by Manning coefficients of
0.020 and 0.028~s~m$^{-1/3}$ for the sandy and coarser bed types. The two
vegetated classes used the \citet{Baptist2007} formulation for submerged
canopy resistance, which derives an equivalent Chezy coefficient from the
canopy height $h_v$, the product of stem density and stem diameter $mD$, the
drag coefficient $C_D$, and the bed Chezy coefficient $C_b$. The sparser class
took $h_v = 0.15$~m, $mD = 3.2$~m$^{-1}$, and $C_D = 1.00$, and the denser
class took $h_v = 0.50$~m, $mD = 5.0$~m$^{-1}$, and $C_D = 0.80$, both with
$C_b = 45$~m$^{1/2}$~s$^{-1}$. Roughness was updated every 300~s.

The spatial assignment of classes was derived from a supervised random-forest
classification of PlanetScope SuperDove imagery acquired in August 2023 at 3~m
ground sampling distance, following the mapping approach of
\citet{Maltese2025}. Classified pixels were transferred to the mesh with a
20~m search radius. The resulting roughness link file must be written in the
same geographic coordinate system as the mesh, because a projected file is
accepted without error while assigning no links at all.

For reference, \citet{Ingrassia2024} obtained a best-fit uniform
Gauckler--Strickler coefficient of 20~m$^{1/3}$~s$^{-1}$ for the vegetated
Stagnone in a depth-averaged model, equivalent to a Manning coefficient of
0.050~s~m$^{-1/3}$.

\subsection{Observational data and validation metrics}
\label{sec:methods_validation}

Water level was recorded at ten-minute resolution at three stations, BocaNord
at the northern inlet, BocaSud near the southern inlet, and AltaVilaEst on the
eastern flank of the central basin. Offshore performance was assessed at the
Marettimo tide gauge. The model cell used for that comparison was not the cell
nearest the gauge, which sits in the lee of the island and carries almost no
free-surface variance. A cell at 12.0753$^\circ$E, 37.9747$^\circ$N with a bed
level below $-0.3$~m was used instead, consistent with earlier versions of the
model.

Water-level skill was quantified with the raw root-mean-square error, the
bias, and the anomaly root-mean-square error computed after removing the
respective means, together with the linear correlation and the ratio of
modelled to observed standard deviation. The three error measures are related
by $\mathrm{RMSE}^2 = \mathrm{bias}^2 + \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{anom}}^2$, so
reporting them jointly separates a constant reference-level offset from
genuine dynamic error in phase and amplitude. Reporting either measure alone
would confound the two. The first simulated day was discarded as spin-up.

Lagrangian skill was assessed against GPS-tracked surface drifters released in
the lagoon interior during the simulation window. Simulated trajectories were
obtained by advecting particles with OpenDrift through the modelled surface
current field. Agreement was quantified with the normalised cumulative
Lagrangian separation skill score of \citet{Liu2011}, which accumulates the
separation between modelled and observed positions and normalises it by the
distance travelled by the observed drifter, and with the endpoint proximity,
the separation between modelled and observed positions at the end of each
deployment. \citet{Revelard2021} discuss the sensitivity of this class of
metric to the normalisation choice in coastal applications.

\subsection{Numerical experiments}
\label{sec:methods_experiments}

Five simulations were run over the same nine-day window, from the same initial
condition and with identical boundary and atmospheric forcing, so that the
differences between them isolate individual model components
(Table~\ref{tab:ensemble}).

Four of the five form a factorial combination of two factors. The first is the
seagrass roughness treatment, either a uniform Manning coefficient of
0.023~s~m$^{-1/3}$ over the whole domain or the spatially distributed
trachytope field of Section~\ref{sec:methods_roughness}. The second is the
morphodynamic bed update, either a fixed bed or a two-fraction sediment
transport and bed-level update with sand and silt. All four include wave
coupling. The fifth simulation repeats the fixed-bed, uniform-roughness member
with the wave model switched off entirely, so that the difference between the
two isolates the contribution of wave coupling under otherwise identical
conditions.

Two aspects of this design deserve statement. The morphodynamic factor is
included as a sensitivity test rather than as a calibrated prediction, because
the critical shear stress for erosion has not been constrained against a
repeated bathymetric survey. Sections~\ref{sec:res_attribution} and
\ref{sec:discussion} treat it accordingly. The wave contribution is assessed
along the fixed-bed, uniform-roughness axis only, so any interaction between
wave forcing and canopy resistance is not resolved by this ensemble.
