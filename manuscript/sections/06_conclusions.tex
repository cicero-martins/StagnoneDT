\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Five configurations of a coupled three-dimensional model of the Stagnone di
Marsala, differing in wave coupling, bed mobility and the spatial treatment of
seagrass resistance, produce water level records that cannot be told apart.
Anomaly RMSE differs by five millimetres or less between them at any station,
and no configuration is consistently best across the three stations. The same
five differ by 30\% in Liu--Weisberg skill and by a factor of 1.7 in endpoint
separation. Water level skill, on its own, certifies a model's capacity to fill
and drain a basin and says little about where the water inside it goes.

Wave coupling did not improve Lagrangian prediction in this basin over this
window. It reduced skill by 0.035, small but negative in eight of twelve
deployments. The measurement applies to summer conditions containing one
moderate swell event and to a fixed bed under uniform roughness, which is the
single axis along which the wave contribution was isolated.

Distributed seagrass roughness and bed mobility each change Lagrangian skill by
an amount indistinguishable from zero when applied alone. Applied together they
raise it by 0.15, and only that combination reproduces the observed drifter
transport rate, at 0.99 of the observed mean speed against 0.76 to 0.85 for the
other four. The two treatments interact.

The mechanism of that interaction is not established here. It is not the final
bed state, whose spatial pattern and magnitude are nearly identical between the
two morphodynamic configurations. It is not suppression of erosion beneath the
canopy, which runs in the opposite direction in both. It is not a stronger mean
current field, since the successful configuration has a lower lagoon-mean
surface speed than two configurations whose particles travel too slowly. The
transport difference is therefore a property of where and when the particles
meet the flow rather than of the strength of the flow, and identifying it
requires a relation between particle position and local current phase that this
work does not provide.

The configuration reproducing observed transport carries an unconstrained
parameter. Its critical shear stress for erosion has not been fixed against a
repeated bathymetric survey, and the resulting bed changes by up to 2.4~m over
nine days in isolated cells. Whether the improvement reflects bed evolution or
compensation for a deficient initial bathymetry cannot be decided without
observational constraint on the bed.

Surface current speed exceeds bed speed by a factor of 1.9 to 4.5 in the lagoon
interior, in a basin of sub-metre mean depth. That ratio itself depends on
whether the bed is allowed to move, rising from near 2.0 with a fixed bed to
near 4.0 with a mobile one, so the vertical structure of this system is not
separable from the sediment dynamics that shape it.
