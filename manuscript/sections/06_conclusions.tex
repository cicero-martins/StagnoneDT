\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Eight configurations of a coupled three-dimensional model of the Stagnone di
Marsala, spanning a complete factorial in wave coupling, bed mobility and the
presence of a seagrass canopy, were evaluated against interior water level
records and 35 GPS surface drifters.

The seagrass canopy is the first-order control on transport in this basin. It
raises Liu--Weisberg skill by 0.087 to 0.256 depending on the configuration it
enters, with every bootstrap interval clear of zero and 26 to 32 of the 35
drifters improving in each of the four contrasts. It is the only one of the
three treatments whose four contrasts share a sign, and it is at least twice the
size of anything else in the design. No canopy parameter was fitted to any
observation used to evaluate the model, the values coming from published flume
and large-scale measurements of the same species scaled by satellite-derived
fractional cover.

An apparent interaction between wave coupling and bed mobility does not survive
the canopy. Without a canopy, each of the two costs skill alone, by 0.035 and
0.071, and together they gain 0.084, an interaction term of $+0.189$ against an
additive prediction of $-0.105$. With the canopy present the same term is
$+0.028$, and both contrasts lose their significance. The standard deviation of
skill across the four cells falls from 0.057 to 0.016. What looked like a
coupling between two processes was largely a compensation for a third that was
missing, and nothing internal to the ensemble distinguished the two readings.
This is a general hazard of factorial attribution rather than a feature of this
basin. An ensemble that omits a first-order process can produce a clean,
significant and physically plausible interaction between the processes it does
include.

Where water level is measured decides whether it can separate such
configurations. At the two stations near the inlets, anomaly RMSE differs by
2.1 and 9.2~mm across the eight members with no consistent ordering, while the
same simulations differ by 0.27 in Liu--Weisberg skill. At the innermost
station, inside the shallow vegetated basin, it differs by 36.2~mm and separates
the vegetated members from the bare ones without overlap, with correlations of
0.94 to 0.98 against 0.78 to 0.82. Water level is not inherently insensitive to
these processes, it is insensitive near the boundary that forces it.

The canopy reshapes the vertical structure of the basin, taking the ratio of
surface to bed speed from 1.85--2.14 to 4.45--5.25 while wave coupling moves it
by 0.2 to 0.8. In a basin of sub-metre mean depth the layer a surface drifter
samples and the layer that governs bed shear stress differ by a factor of five,
so a depth-averaged representation of this system would not merely lose vertical
resolution, it would lose the process that governs where material goes.

Two limitations bound these conclusions. The canopy enters the momentum equation
of the flow rather than the roughness field passed to the wave model, so the
waves in these simulations propagate over an effectively bare bed and the wave
contrasts are conditional on that. And reconfiguration is represented through
mechanical bending rather than through the empirical velocity-dependent
formulation that flume measurements support, which the model offers only in its
depth-averaged solver. The bending does respond to load, but a 13\% change in
the tabulated leaf density moves the velocity field by 20\% at the upper end of
the range, so that parameter deserves measurement in this meadow. None of the
three bears on the canopy contrasts, which are measured on the flow.
