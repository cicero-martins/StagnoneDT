\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Eight configurations of a coupled three-dimensional model of the Stagnone di
Marsala, spanning a complete factorial in wave coupling, bed mobility and the
spatial treatment of seagrass resistance, produce water level records that
cannot be told apart. Anomaly RMSE differs by at most 4.2~mm between them at any
station, and no configuration is consistently best across the three stations.
The same eight differ by 50\% in Liu--Weisberg skill and by a factor of 1.9 in
endpoint separation. Water level skill, on its own, certifies a model's capacity
to fill and drain a basin and says little about where the water inside it goes.

Neither wave coupling nor bed mobility improves Lagrangian prediction on its
own, and each is what makes the other work. Wave coupling on a fixed bed costs
0.035 to 0.042 in skill. Bed mobility without waves costs 0.071 to 0.075 and
makes every one of the 35 drifters worse in both roughness treatments. Applied
together they gain 0.119 to 0.232. The two worst and the two best members of the
ensemble are built from the same pair of treatments and differ only in whether
both are switched on.

Distributed seagrass roughness is a third-order refinement on this evidence. It
changes skill by 0.009 or less in three of its four conditions, and by 0.081 in
the fourth, which is on a mobile bed with wave coupling. Its effect on the wave
field is nonetheless real and ordered by canopy density, reducing mean orbital
velocity at the bed by 14.3\% over \emph{P.\ oceanica} against 3.3\% over
exposed rock.

Separating the trajectory error into magnitude and direction shows the two
moving together rather than independently. The no-wave mobile-bed members are
worst on both, at 0.73 of the observed path length and 19.8$^\circ$ of heading
error; the fixed-bed members sit at 0.77 to 0.80 and 15.8 to 16.2$^\circ$; and
the wave-coupled mobile-bed members reach 0.84 and 11.3$^\circ$, and 0.88 and
8.5$^\circ$ with distributed roughness. Every treatment that improves the
trajectory improves both parts of the error, which is what would be expected if
they act on the velocity field as a whole.

Neither treatment can therefore be assessed on its own. A wave contribution
measured on a fixed bed, or a bed contribution measured without waves, describes
a corner of the design rather than the treatment, and the conditions under which
an effect was measured belong with the effect.

The configuration reproducing observed transport carries an unconstrained
parameter. Its critical shear stress for erosion has not been fixed against a
repeated bathymetric survey, and the lagoon bed changes by up to 1.4~m over nine
days in isolated cells against a median of 0.05~m. Whether the improvement
reflects bed evolution or compensation for an outdated initial bathymetry cannot
be decided without observational constraint on the bed, and satellite-derived
bathymetry over repeated epochs is the most tractable route to it.

Surface current speed exceeds bed speed by a factor of 1.85 to 2.20 in the
lagoon interior, in a basin of sub-metre mean depth, and that ratio is set by
wave coupling rather than by bed mobility. The vertical structure of this system
therefore responds to the same model choice that dominates its transport.
