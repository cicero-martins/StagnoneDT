\begin{abstract}
%% 197 words. CHECK the ECSS Guide for Authors limit before submission. Do not
%% assume the 150-word ENVSOFT cap that applies to the reservoirs manuscripts.
Coastal lagoon models are routinely validated against water level, yet water
level may constrain transport weakly. We test this in the Stagnone di Marsala,
a micro-tidal Sicilian lagoon of sub-metre mean depth colonised by
\emph{Posidonia oceanica}, using a three-dimensional Delft3D Flexible Mesh
model coupled to SWAN over nine days of July 2025. Five configurations differ
in wave coupling, bed mobility and whether seagrass resistance is uniform or
distributed from a satellite classification, and each is scored against three
tide gauges and 35 GPS surface drifters. The configurations are
indistinguishable in water level, with anomaly RMSE differing by five
millimetres or less, while their Liu--Weisberg skill spans 0.505 to 0.659 and
their endpoint separation 343 to 583~m. Wave coupling reduces Lagrangian skill
by 0.035. Distributed roughness and bed mobility each change it negligibly on
their own but raise it by 0.15 together, and only that combination reproduces
the observed drifter transport rate. The mechanism of the interaction is not
the modelled bed change, the suppression of erosion under the canopy, or a
stronger mean current, and remains unidentified. Surface speed exceeds bed
speed by 1.9 to 4.5 times, a vertical structure that itself depends on bed
mobility.
\end{abstract}
