\begin{abstract}
%% 240 words. ECSS limit is 250 (confirmed 2026-08-07). Not the 150-word ENVSOFT
%% cap that applies to the reservoirs manuscripts.
Coastal lagoon models are routinely validated against water level, while the
quantity governing habitat connectivity and dispersal is transport. We ask
which modelled processes control transport in a shallow vegetated
lagoon, and whether water level can identify them. A three-dimensional Delft3D
Flexible Mesh model of the Stagnone di Marsala, a micro-tidal Sicilian lagoon of
sub-metre mean depth colonised by \emph{Posidonia oceanica}, was coupled to SWAN
over nine days of July 2025. Eight configurations form a complete
factorial in wave coupling, bed mobility and seagrass canopy, the canopy
parameterised from published measurement and distributed by satellite
classification. Each was scored against three tide gauges and 35 GPS
surface drifters held out of every model decision. Liu--Weisberg skill spans
0.469 to 0.735, and the ensemble divides on the canopy alone, which raises skill
by 0.087 to 0.256 with every bootstrap interval clear of zero. Without the
canopy, wave coupling and bed mobility each reduce skill alone yet gain it
together, an interaction of $+0.189$ that falls to $+0.028$ once the canopy is
present. Water level separates the configurations only at the gauge inside the
vegetated basin, by 36~mm in anomaly RMSE against 2 and 9~mm near the inlets.
The canopy is therefore the first-order control on transport here, and the
apparent synergy between waves and bed mobility was compensation for its
absence. Factorial attribution can yield a clean, plausible interaction that is
an artefact of an omitted process, detectable only against an observation type
the calibration did not use.
\end{abstract}
