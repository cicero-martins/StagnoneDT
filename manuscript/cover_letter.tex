\vspace{0.5cm}
Dear Editors,

\vspace{0.2cm}
We submit for your consideration the manuscript \textbf{``Transport validation
of a wave-coupled lagoon model: interacting effects of seagrass roughness and
bed mobility''}, by Cicero Martins Jr., Giuseppe Ciraolo and Mauro De Marchis,
as an original research paper.

The corresponding author is MSc.\ Cicero Martins Jr.

\vspace{0.3cm}
Coastal lagoon models are commonly validated against water level and then used
to describe transport. This manuscript asks whether the first licenses the
second. We built six configurations of a three-dimensional Delft3D Flexible
Mesh model coupled online with SWAN, differing in wave coupling, bed mobility
and whether seagrass resistance is uniform or distributed from a satellite
classification, and scored each against three tide gauges and 35 GPS surface
drifters over the same nine days.

The configurations are indistinguishable in water level. Their anomaly RMSE
differs by six millimetres or less at any station, which is within measurement
uncertainty, so a calibration conducted on water level alone would have no
basis on which to prefer any of them. Over the same simulations their
Liu--Weisberg skill spans 0.505 to 0.655 and their endpoint separation 334 to
661~m.

What separates them is an interaction rather than any single process.
Distributed seagrass roughness and bed mobility each change Lagrangian skill by
an amount indistinguishable from zero when applied alone, and together they
raise it by 0.13 to 0.15. Decomposing the trajectory error into a magnitude
part and a direction part shows why: bed mobility restores the distance
travelled, from 0.79 to 0.94 of observed, while distributed roughness reduces
the mean heading error from 12.5 to 8.4 degrees and does so only once the bed
can move. We report this as a narrowing rather than a complete mechanism, and
we state the three candidate explanations we tested and had to reject.

We believe the work suits \textit{Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science} on two
grounds. It is a study of brackish water and lagoon phenomena and of coastal
sedimentary processes, both within the journal's stated scope. And although the
model is built for one basin, the question it answers is not local: the finding
concerns what a class of validation data can and cannot certify about a model,
and the magnitude-direction decomposition we introduce is applicable wherever
drifter observations and a circulation model exist together. The prior study
closest to our wave treatment, Carniello et al.\ (2011), appeared in this
journal.

We also draw your attention to two results we report against our own interest.
Wave coupling, which we introduce to this system for the first time, does not
improve Lagrangian prediction over this window. And the configuration that best
reproduces observed transport depends on a morphodynamic parameter we have not
been able to constrain against a repeated bathymetric survey, which we state
explicitly as a limitation rather than leaving to a reader to discover.
