\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In a Mediterranean lagoon of sub-metre mean depth whose floor is largely
seagrass, a canopy drag resolved over the plant height is the first-order
control on modelled Lagrangian transport. It raises Liu--Weisberg skill by
0.120 without wave coupling and by 0.114 with it, against $-0.022$ and
$-0.028$ for wave coupling, and the two factors do not interact. The canopy
acts through the shape of the velocity profile, the ratio of surface to bed
speed rising from about 2 to about 5 while bed shear stress falls by a factor
of 11.

The penalty of wave coupling follows largely from a wave field computed over a
bare bed. With the canopy also dissipating wave energy it falls to $-0.005$,
and the canopy contrast under wave coupling rises to $+0.137$. The size of the
canopy effect is conditional on the state the model reaches, since a uniform
offshore start raises the canopy contrasts to about 0.19 by degrading the bare
members alone.

Across the four members, the anomaly RMSE of water level differs by 1.6~mm at
the South Mouth and by 12~mm at the North Mouth, where it follows wave
coupling, and by 32~mm at AltaVilaEst, where it follows the canopy and
correlation rises from 0.79--0.81 to 0.94--0.95, so water level identifies the
canopy only where it is measured inside the vegetated basin, and validation at
the inlets alone would not have identified it.

With the mapped meadow in place, bed-layer speed in the lagoon interior falls
by about 72\% and the Lagrangian skill of vegetated and bare members no longer
overlaps, so the transport of a shallow basin of this type depends on its
meadow as much as on its geometry and forcing.
