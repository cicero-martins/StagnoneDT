\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In a shallow Mediterranean lagoon whose floor is largely seagrass and whose
mean depth is below one metre, the canopy emerges from this ensemble as the
first-order control on Lagrangian transport. Representing it raises
Liu--Weisberg skill by 0.120 without wave coupling and by 0.114 with it, both
bootstrap intervals clear of zero, against $-0.022$ and $-0.028$ for wave
coupling, and the two factors do not interact. When the canopy also dissipates
wave energy, the wave penalty under the canopy falls to $-0.005$ and the canopy
contrast with waves rises to 0.137. The canopy acts through the shape of the
velocity profile, more than doubling the ratio of surface to bed speed while
lowering bed shear stress by a factor of 11, and with every parameter taken from
published measurement the gain follows from where the drag is applied rather
than from calibration.

Anomaly RMSE of water level differs across the four members by 1.6~mm at the
South Mouth, by 12~mm at the North Mouth, where it follows wave coupling, and
by 32~mm at the gauge inside the vegetated basin, where it follows the canopy
and correlation rises from 0.79--0.81 to 0.94--0.95. The position of a gauge
therefore decides what it can constrain, and validation at the inlets alone
would not have identified the canopy.

The size of the canopy effect is conditional on the state the model reaches, a
uniform offshore start raising the canopy contrasts to about 0.19 by degrading
the bare members alone. The meadow of this basin is itself changing, and
between the two arms of the design, one carrying it as mapped and the other
none of it, bed-layer speed in the lagoon interior falls by about 72\%, the
surface layer moves from about twice the speed of the bed to about five times,
and Lagrangian skill separates without overlap. The transport regime of a basin
of this type is therefore a property of its meadow as much as of its geometry
and its forcing.
