\vspace{0.5cm}
Dear Editors,

\vspace{0.2cm}
The manuscript \textbf{``Seagrass canopy drag and wave coupling in the Lagrangian transport of a shallow lagoon''}, by Cicero Martins Jr., Mauro De Marchis, Giuseppe Ciraolo, Carmelo Nasello, Antonino Maltese, is
submitted for consideration as an original research paper. The corresponding
author is MSc.\ Cicero Martins Jr.

\vspace{0.3cm}
Coastal lagoon models are commonly validated against water level and then used
to describe transport. The manuscript asks which modelled processes control
transport in a shallow vegetated lagoon and whether water level can identify
them. A factorial in wave coupling and seagrass canopy gives four
configurations of a three-dimensional Delft3D Flexible Mesh model coupled
online with SWAN, each scored against three tide gauges and 35 GPS surface
drifters over the same nine days.

The seagrass canopy raises Liu--Weisberg skill by 0.120 and
0.114, with both bootstrap intervals clear of zero, against $-0.022$ and
$-0.028$ for wave coupling, and with the canopy also dissipating wave energy the
latter falls to $-0.005$. The canopy is represented as a three-dimensional
drag field whose parameters come from published measurement of the two species,
with nothing fitted. Water level separates the canopy treatments only at the
gauge inside the vegetated basin, where anomaly RMSE differs by 26 and 27~mm,
against at most 4~mm at the two gauges near the inlets.

The canopy acts through the vertical structure of the flow, more than doubling
the ratio of surface to bed speed, which is what relocates a surface drifter. A
start from a uniform offshore column inflates the canopy effect from 0.12 to
0.19 by degrading the bare configurations alone, so the size of such a
contrast depends on what else the model gets wrong.

The work falls within the scope of \textit{Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf
Science} as a study of lagoon phenomena. Although the model is built for one
basin, the questions it answers concern what a class of validation data can
certify and where a gauge must sit to discriminate between model treatments,
and both apply wherever drifter observations and a circulation model exist
together. The prior study closest to the wave treatment, Carniello et al.\
(2011), appeared in this journal. The limitations are stated in the
manuscript, including the fixed bed and the single nine-day window.
